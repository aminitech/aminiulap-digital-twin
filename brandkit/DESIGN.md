## 1. Brand Overview

Amini provides **InfraTech solutions** to close data and compute gaps for Africa and the Global South. Every surface — product UI, deck, doc, or web page — must read as **premium, technical, and forward-looking**.

| Principle | What it means in practice |
| --------- | ------------------------- |
| **Premium** | Generous space, precise alignment, no clutter. Restraint over decoration. |
| **Technical** | Monospace labels, clear data hierarchy, honest structure. |
| **Forward-looking** | Dark-first, high contrast, confident use of a single warm accent. |

> **Default to dark mode.** Space Black backgrounds, Marble White text, Sunglow accent — used sparingly.

## 2. Core Message

> *Audience-independent. This is the spine — every segment in §3 is a lens on it, never a contradiction of it.*

### Category — Infra-tech

Amini is an **infra-tech** company. The exclusion list is the most useful definition we have — keep it verbatim:

> **Not** a telco · **not** an insights company · **not** SaaS · **not** public cloud.

### The core message

> ### We build the physical and digital infrastructure to transition 4 billion people into the AI economy.

**The analogy that works:** every country had to connect to the internet in the 1990s; today every country must connect to AI — or be left behind.

### Origin story — a deeper realisation, not a pivot

Use this exact framing. It defuses the *"why did you change what you do"* question investors and partners will ask.

> The problem was never insights — it was **infrastructure**.

### The three divides

The strongest structural proof point. Appears verbatim on the website and in the MIT Solve entry.

| Divide | The gap |
| ------ | ------- |
| **Data** | The data to build with is fragmented, unusable, or absent. |
| **Connectivity** | The pipes to move it don't reach. |
| **Compute** | **Africa holds under 1% of global compute.** ← anchor stat |

### Sovereignty, defined

Governments ask exactly these four questions. Answer them directly:

1. **Who built it, and where does it sit?**
2. **How do our country and citizens benefit?**
3. **Where is the value generated?**
4. **What happens when we want to leave?**

> **Critical caveat — say it out loud, early, every time: sovereignty is not isolation.**
> The Macron framing gives cover — *"shared sovereignty,"* African, European, and Taiwanese companies building together.

## 3. Segmented Messaging

> *Same spine, three lenses. Lead with the value each audience actually buys.*
> ⚠️ **Internal** — the "what's not working" notes and switching-cost strategy are diagnostics for us, never customer-facing lines.

### 3.1 Government / public sector — *primary*

**Lead with agency, not technology.** The phrase that works (Barbados): *"a trust layer with local data control."*

**What's demonstrably working**

- **Named, phased workflows over platform tours.** BAJAN-X progressed once we mapped a prioritised list of agencies and workflows. Origin stalled when pitched as an abstract "data platform" — it needs user personas (*data stewards*, not "government").
- **Switching costs framed as sovereignty.** *(Internal:* the 80% civil-servant-adoption target raises switching costs against competitors.*)* To the minister it is simply: **"your people own this."**
- **Digitisation as the wedge.** Land records, birth records, civil registry, Supreme Court documents, 1.27M police records. The most legible thing Amini does — it turns sovereignty from an abstraction into a filing cabinet.
- **Security posture as a door-opener.** CISO approval, maturity assessments for integration partners, Tier 3 target, PII encryption. Governments read this as seriousness.

**What's not working — the lesson**

> **Do not let "sovereign" be heard as "worse."** The Benin pilot exposed the gap between promise and artefact (97/109 documents fully ingested, metadata loss, stale-cache answers; users benchmarking against ChatGPT on legal reasoning and citations). The right message is the debrief's own conclusion: **transparency about document quality is vital.**

**Proof points to lead with:** Barbados (5+ government domains, 2 core platforms) · Minister Reid's testimonial · the police command centre · Trident ID.

**Entry point (the ladder):**

> **Soma** *(digitisation)* → **Origin** *(governance)* → **Bridge / GovChat** *(use)* → **Pods / Ulap** *(own it)*

### 3.2 Investors & partners

**Lead with the moat — which is not the software.**

1. **Sovereign-by-default architecture** that hyperscalers structurally cannot offer.
2. **Government relationships at ministerial level** across four countries.
3. **The Foxconn / Bull alliance** — Foxconn's first dedicated African infrastructure programme, with Amini as strategic partner across Africa and emerging economies.

**Market framing (already in circulation):** Africa's digital economy projected at **$1.5T by 2030**; the data-centre market from **$3.49B (2024) → $6.81B (2030)**, installed capacity tripling to **3,460+ MW**.

**Credibility stack:** TIME100 AI · MIT Solve '24 · WEF Technology Pioneer · Kofi Annan Award · One Young World · AWS Compute for Climate '25. Plus **SIDSense on arXiv** — 94.2% sensing accuracy, 23ms mean decision latency, field-validated in Barbados. *Peer-reviewable research is rare in this category — use it harder than you do.*

**The model that makes economics legible:** the **BOT model** (Togo, 27 ministries) — you don't sell a licence, you **build, operate, and transfer**.

### 3.3 Telco / commercial

**Lead with AI-RAN and private networks as *sovereign connectivity* — not a telco play.** *"Never a telco"* matters here: Amini is the layer **beneath**, not the competitor.

**Proof:** two years of AI/ML–RAN research · AI-RAN Alliance membership · the Barbados private-5G pilot around the police command centre · SIDSense's maritime deployment · spectrum licence held.

**Commercial (mining, agri, financial):** the Geothax pattern — **unify fragmented datasets to de-risk capital decisions.** Here sovereignty is *secondary*; the primary win is that **the data was unusable and now isn't.** **Do not lead with sovereignty to a mining customer.**

- All text must meet **WCAG AA** contrast against its background.

## 5. Typography

### Primary — Helvetica Now Display
- On **dark** backgrounds: **Regular** weight
- On **light** backgrounds: **Medium** weight
- Enable stylistic alternates: `ss01`, `ss02`, `ss05`, `ss07`

### Secondary — Geist Mono
- Labels, eyebrows, navigation, UI labels
- **ALL-CAPS** at small sizes
- Letter-spacing **4–6%**

### Fallback (open-source) — Geist / Aspekta
Use if Helvetica Now is unavailable. The Aspekta variable family (weights 50–1000) ships in the brand assets and is a suitable open-source geometric substitute for display use.

### Type Scale

| Element | Size | Tracking | Line Height | Notes |
| ------- | ---- | :------: | :---------: | ----- |
| **H1** | 80px / 5rem | −3% | 110% | Display |
| **H4** | 32px / 2rem | 1% | — | |
| **Section Title** | 20px / 1.25rem | 1% | — | |
| **Body** | 16px / 1rem | 1% | 150% | |
| **Small Body** | 14px / 0.875rem | 2% | 150% | |
| **Navigation / Eyebrow** | 12px / 0.75rem | 6% | — | Geist Mono, ALL-CAPS |
| **UI Labels** | 14px / 0.875rem | 4% | — | Geist Mono, ALL-CAPS |

**Hierarchy:** `eyebrow › heading › body › caption` — always in that order.

- Favicon / 1:1 mark: use the icon monogram (`icon-black.svg` / `icon-white.svg`).

## 7. Layout Principles

- Generous **white space** and clean **grid** structures.
- Section labels / eyebrows in **Geist Mono, ALL-CAPS**, small size.
- Headings in **Helvetica Now Display** (or Geist) with tight letter-spacing.
- Body text at a comfortable reading width with **150%** line-height.
- Maintain visual hierarchy: **eyebrow › heading › body › caption**.

## 8. Design Tokens

Drop-in values for CSS / design tools.

```css
:root {
  /* Primary */
  --color-space-black:   #121212; /* primary background (dark mode default) */
  --color-marble-white:  #FFFFFF; /* primary text on dark */
  --color-sunglow:       #FFC83C; /* accent / highlights / CTA — never a fill bg */

  --font-display: "Helvetica Now Display", "Geist", "Aspekta", system-ui, sans-serif;
  --font-mono:    "Geist Mono", ui-monospace, "SFMono-Regular", monospace;

  --text-eyebrow: 0.75rem;   /* 12px, Geist Mono, ALL-CAPS, tracking 6% */
}
```

## 9. Constraints & Do / Don't

**Hard constraints**

- Never deviate from the color palette.
- Never use fonts outside Helvetica Now Display, Geist Mono, or Geist / Aspekta.
- Dark mode is the default unless light mode is explicitly requested.
- All text must meet accessibility contrast ratios against its background.

| ✅ Do | 🚫 Don't |
| ----- | -------- |
| Use Space Black backgrounds by default | Use Sunglow as a background fill |
| Reserve Sunglow for accents, CTAs, highlights | Put Bordeaux on a dark background |
| Use white logo on dark, black on light | Distort, rotate, outline, or recolor the logo |
| Set labels/eyebrows in Geist Mono ALL-CAPS | Introduce off-palette colors |
| Keep generous clear space around the logo | Crowd the logo or drop it on low-contrast fills |
