The core message

We build the physical and digital infrastructure to transition 4 billion people into the AI economy.

The analogy that works: every country had to connect to the internet in the 1990s; today every country must connect to AI — or be left behind.

Origin story — a deeper realisation, not a pivot The problem was never insights — it was infrastructure. Use this exact framing; it defuses the “why did you change what you do” question.

The three divides — the structural proof point

Data

The data to build with is fragmented, unusable, or absent.

Connectivity

The pipes to move it don't reach.

Compute · anchor stat
<1%

Africa holds under 1% of global compute.

Sovereignty, defined — governments ask exactly these

01

Who built it, and where does it sit?

02

03

Where is the value generated?

04

What happens when we want to leave?

Critical caveat — say it out loud, early, every time Sovereignty is not isolation. The Macron framing gives cover — “shared sovereignty,” African, European, and Taiwanese companies building together.

Segmented messaging

Same spine, three lenses. Lead with the value each audience actually buys.

Internal The “what's not working” notes and switching-cost strategy are diagnostics for us — never customer-facing lines.
Government / public sector · primary

Lead with agency, not technology.

The phrase that works: “a trust layer with local data control.”

What's demonstrably working

Named, phased workflows over platform tours. Map a prioritised list of agencies and workflows. Origin needs personas — data stewards, not “government.”
Switching costs framed as sovereignty. To the minister it is simply: “your people own this.”
Digitisation as the wedge. Land, birth, civil registry, Supreme Court, 1.27M police records — sovereignty becomes a filing cabinet.
Security posture as a door-opener. CISO approval, maturity assessments, Tier 3 target, PII encryption.
What's not working — the lesson Do not let “sovereign” be heard as “worse.” Benin exposed the gap between promise and artefact (97/109 documents ingested, metadata loss, stale-cache answers). The right message: transparency about document quality is vital.

05

Logo

A custom geometric sans-serif wordmark paired with a bold “A” monogram in a softened square. White on dark, black on light — never a low-contrast pairing.

White · on dark · default
Black · on light

Monogram + wordmark

Do

  • Use the white logo on dark, the black logo on light.
  • Maintain the required clear space around the logo at all times.
  • Use the favicon / 1:1 mark for app icons and avatars.

Don't

  • Distort, outline, add drop shadows, or rotate the logo.
  • Recolor off-palette or drop it on low-contrast fills.
  • Crowd the mark or scale the wordmark below legible size.
06

Layout

Generous white space and clean grid structures. Maintain the hierarchy: eyebrow › heading › body › caption.

Section labels and eyebrows in Geist Mono, ALL-CAPS, small size.
Headings in Helvetica Now Display (or Geist) with tight letter-spacing.
Body text at a comfortable reading width with 150% line-height.
Maintain a clear visual hierarchy — eyebrow › heading › body › caption.
